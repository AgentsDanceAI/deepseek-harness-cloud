# Future source-available business requirements

**Status: non-operative product requirements.** This file is not a license and
does not modify the AGPL-3.0-only terms currently governing this repository.

## Intended future permissions

- Internal use by one organization, including modification and deployment for
  that organization's own personnel.
- Evaluation, security review, education, and non-commercial experimentation,
  subject to future lawyer-approved text.
- A useful Community edition that can be built, tested, secured, self-hosted,
  upgraded, backed up, restored, and used without a private license service.

## Intended future commercial-authorization triggers

Counsel may evaluate narrowly drafted conditions for:

- operating the software as a managed, multi-tenant service for unrelated
  customers;
- reselling or white-labeling the product; and
- removing attribution or branding when future lawyer-approved text expressly
  requires it.

These intentions are not restrictions under the repository's current license.

## Non-negotiable transition constraints

- Rights already received under AGPL-3.0 remain available for the versions
  distributed under AGPL-3.0.
- New terms can apply only prospectively and only to material the licensor has
  authority to relicense.
- Existing Community features are not removed merely to place them behind a
  commercial license.
- Security fixes, data export, backups, upgrade safety, and the supported
  self-host path are not paywalled.
- A qualified lawyer must draft or approve the operative license, notices,
  contribution terms, compatibility analysis, and public naming.
- Public materials must use “source available” or “Open Core” accurately and
  must not call a non-OSI-approved license “open source.”

## Proposed edition principle

Hosted value comes from operating the service: managed capacity, upgrades,
backups, monitoring, abuse response, billing operations, and support. Enterprise
value may come from net-new governance, compliance, scale, integration, and
commercial-rights extensions. Neither category retroactively changes the license
or classification of published Community capabilities.
